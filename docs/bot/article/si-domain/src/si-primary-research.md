# SI domain article: primary-source verification

Task: 20261005-007. Checked 2026-10-05 UTC. This is a concise evidence ledger, not legal advice. Source text is paraphrased; full articles, raw response logs, and personal registrant data are not reproduced.

## 1. Federal terminology change — verified

- **S1, White House executive order, 2026-09-29:** [Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/). Executive Order **14434** directs executive departments/agencies to substitute Super Intelligence/SI for Artificial Intelligence/AI in official communications, websites, reports, policy documents and other non-statutory documents, to the extent permitted by law. It does not require rewriting historical documents. Section 3 initially uses the existing statutory AI definition and gives the science adviser 60 days to propose legislative language.
- **S2, White House fact sheet, 2026-09-29:** [Requested fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/09/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-inaugurates-the-era-of-super-intelligence/). Directly accessible; corroborates the terminology policy.
- **S3, Federal Register, published 2026-10-02:** [Document 2026-20321](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20321/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence), **91 FR 63129–63130**. The [official GovInfo PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-02/pdf/2026-20321.pdf) was also read and matches the order.

**Editorial limit:** This is an executive-branch terminology policy, not an order requiring private companies or the whole world to rename AI. A political label is not technical evidence that superintelligence has been achieved.

## 2. Registry growth — directly verified

- **S4, Register.si:** [Follow the Growth of .si Domains](https://www.register.si/en/news/follow-the-growth-of-si-domains/). The public page's embedded chart data was inspected on 2026-10-05; this is the registry's own series, not a secondary estimate. Monthly data: **August 2026: 3,515; September 2026: 46,066**. The [registry homepage](https://www.register.si/en/) independently displays 46,066 for the previous month.
- Calculation: 46,066 / 3,515 = **13.10555 times**; increase = **1,210.55%**, or approximately **13.1 times / +1,211%**. Do not call this a hundredfold monthly increase.
- Selected calendar-day observations from S4: September 19 **143**; September 20 **928**; September 21 **543**; September 22 **2,139**; September 23 **4,478**; September 29 **4,115**; September 30 **11,331**; October 1 **12,728**; October 2 **12,678**; October 3 **11,043**.
- The same monthly series records May **6,342** and June **6,938**, showing that increased interest did not begin from zero in late September.

**Editorial limits:** September includes registrations before the September 29 order. Calendar-day values differ from the homepage's rolling last-24-hours count. Registrations are not counts of operating websites, deployed AI services, unique buyers, completed resales or economic profit. Prefer the stable monthly comparison over volatile homepage totals.

## 3. Official registry stance — verified

- **S5, Register.si, undated page read 2026-10-05:** [Number of .si domains exceeds 200,000](https://www.register.si/obvestila/stevilo-domen-pod-si-preseglo-200-000/). It says registrations had risen over preceding months and the AI-naming debate also stimulated interest. Register.si operates within ARNES; customers use registrars. Its uniform registry registration fee is **€10**; registrars set customer prices. It rejects media speculation about millions in earnings for Slovenia and emphasizes operational safety, stability and reliability.

**Editorial limit:** A fixed wholesale fee is not a verified retail price, government profit margin or basis for attributing all additional registrations to one speech/order.

## 5. What .si is, who may register, and what .com means

- **S9, IANA:** [.si delegation](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/si.html), record updated 2026-03-25, identifies a country-code TLD managed by ARNES; registration date 1992-04-01. [.com delegation](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/com.html), updated 2026-03-10, identifies a generic TLD managed by VeriSign Global Registry Services.
- **S10, registry explanation:** [Trademarks, geographical indications and internet domains](https://www.register.si/en/blogs/trademarks-geographical-indications-and-internet-domains/) says an available .si name can be registered by anyone, on a first-come basis; registration does not confer a trademark or override existing third-party rights.
- **S11, official registrar confirmation:** [Dynadot .si eligibility FAQ](https://www.dynadot.com/domain/si) explicitly says eligibility is not restricted by Slovenian residence, citizenship or business registration. This is the registrar's first-party service description, corroborating the registry's general eligibility statement. Do not repeat its broad marketing phrase about having no legal restrictions: trademark and registration rules still apply.
- **S12, registry terms:** [General terms](https://www.register.si/en/general-terms/), page header states publication/validity 2024-11-15. Sections 2.7, 5.2 and 8 define natural/legal-person holders, first-come processing and lawful/good-faith registration obligations.

**Critical distinction:** **spacexsi.com is a .com name whose second-level label contains “si”; it is not a .si registration.** It is outside the Slovenian registration totals and .si ARDS scope. The political naming theme may connect the stories, but the TLDs and dispute systems remain separate.

## 6. Disputes, trademarks and resale — official sources

- **S13, .si ARDS:** [Rules of procedure](https://www.register.si/en/ards-rules-of-procedure/), especially sections 2.2 and 17.3; [registry guidelines](https://www.register.si/en/adr-procedure-guidelines/); S12 section 18. A successful complaint requires a qualifying right protected in Slovenia, absence of the holder's legitimate interest, and bad-faith registration **or** use. All elements require evidence. The guidelines are explanatory, not binding substitutes for the rules.
- **S14, .com UDRP:** [ICANN policy](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/policy-2024-02-21-en), updated 2024-02-21; mandatory implementation by 2025-08-21. Paragraph 4(a) requires trademark identity/confusing similarity, no registrant rights/legitimate interests, and bad-faith registration **and** use. Paragraph 4(b)(i) identifies targeted registration primarily to resell to the trademark owner/competitor above documented domain-related costs as a possible bad-faith circumstance. [ICANN scope explanation](https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/consensus-policies/uniform-domain-name-dispute-resolution-policy/domain-name-dispute-resolution-policies-25-02-2012-en) explicitly includes .com among gTLDs using UDRP.
- **S15, WIPO Overview 3.0, section 3.1.1:** [Official panel-view overview](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/domain-name-disputes/overview/3.0). Registration for resale at a profit does not, by itself, establish bad faith. Context matters: trademark targeting, distinctiveness, knowledge and actual use. A legitimate independent interest can make a sale offer consistent with good faith; targeting a famous mark creates substantial risk.

**Editorial rule:** A sale landing page or asking price supports “offered for sale,” not “sold,” “the registrant made this amount,” or “proven malicious cybersquatting.” Do not determine a particular registrant's legal liability from the asking price alone. The .si **or** test and UDRP **and** test should not be conflated.
